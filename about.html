ABOUT

Taught by someone who still ships code.

Workbench started as a side project and became the main one: teaching development the way it's actually done on the job.

JD

FIG. 05 — THE PITCH

Most courses teach syntax. Few teach judgment.

I spent years building software for small businesses before I started teaching — booking systems, dashboards, storefronts, the unglamorous stuff that actually runs a company. Workbench exists because I kept meeting talented beginners who could write a loop but had never shipped anything real, and kept meeting employers who cared more about the second thing.

Every course here is built backward from an actual client deliverable. You don't learn a framework in the abstract — you learn it by using it to solve the same kind of problem I get paid to solve.

FIG. 06 — HOW WE GOT HERE

A short timeline.

2019

Freelance studio opens

Started building small web apps for local businesses — the projects that later became course material.

2021

First cohort, four students

Ran the HTML & CSS course as a favor for a friend's younger sibling. Word got around.

2023

Six-course catalog

Formalized the curriculum from Foundation through Capstone, each still tied to a real build.

2025

240+ students trained

Started taking on select client briefs specifically so students could work them as capstones.

FIG. 07 — HOW WE TEACH

Four rules that don't change.

Real projects, not toy ones

If a client wouldn't pay for it, we don't assign it.

Small cohorts

Every commit gets a real review from a person, not a rubric.

You keep everything

Your repo, your deployment, your call on how it's built.

Ship, then reflect

You explain your own decisions before we explain ours.
